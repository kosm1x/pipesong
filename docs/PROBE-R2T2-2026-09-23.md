## The model

- Streaming ASR, 2.04 B params (BF16), a fine-tune of Qwen3-ASR-1.7B.
- Decoding chunks from 80 ms to 2 s. The model card claims 200–600 ms average latency.
- **Tuned for Chinese and English.** Spanish is a secondary "cross-lingual" language, and the card publishes no Spanish numbers.
- **Runtime:**
  - Streaming needs vLLM + GPU.
  - The transformers backend (CPU) transcribes offline only.
  - The llama.cpp route (`r2t2_llama`) ships CUDA-built libs only. For CPU, rebuild the pybind extension at llama.cpp commit `ad6c6683` with `-DGGML_CUDA` off (cp312).

## Where R2T2 could fit

- On `flux-pipecat1x`, Deepgram **Flux** does three jobs at once: transcription, end-of-turn detection and barge-in (`src/pipesong/pipeline.py:90`).
- Swapping in R2T2 therefore also needs:
  - turn detection (Silero VAD + Smart-Turn)
  - a custom Pipecat STT service over R2T2's `ws_server.py`
- The roadmap's "local faster-whisper fallback" exists only in `benchmarks/scripts/stt_validation.py`. It is not wired into the pipeline.
- Candidate roles for R2T2:
  - fill that empty local-fallback slot
  - replace Deepgram spend ($0.0078/min Flux)

Either role needs the GPU box that vLLM + Kokoro already need.

## Gates (registered before running)

**Gate 1 — CPU, $0.** Offline Spanish WER on the 100 phone-quality clips:

- Clips: `benchmarks/audio/samples/phone_*.wav`, 8 kHz mu-law resampled to 16 kHz; 5 synthetic TTS voices × 20 sentences.
- References: `benchmarks/prompts/tts_sentences_20.txt`.
- Comparison: faster-whisper large-v3-turbo, CPU int8.
- Normalization: lowercase, punctuation stripped. An accent-stripped WER is reported too.
- **KILL** if R2T2 WER is more than 1 pt (absolute) above whisper-turbo, or above 10 %. Offline WER bounds streaming WER.

**Gate 2 — only after a gate 1 pass.** Streaming chunk latency, real-time factor and streaming WER. Needs the GPU window or the CPU `r2t2_llama` rebuild.

## Gate 1 result: PASS

| Engine | WER | Accent-stripped WER | RTF (2 CPU threads, offline) | Clips wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R2T2 | **4.66 %** | 4.39 % | 1.52 | 20 |
| whisper-large-v3-turbo int8 | 6.76 % | 6.62 % | 2.10 | 23 |

- **Verdict:** R2T2 is 2.1 pts better than whisper-turbo and under 10 %, so the rule passes.
- **R2T2 WER by voice:** fish 5.41 %, dora 5.07 %, alex 5.41 %, santa 1.35 %, xtts 6.08 %.
- **Clip overlap:** 5 clips wrong only in R2T2, 8 only in whisper, 15 in both.
- **Most errors are number formatting** ("15" vs "quince"). Clips with digits make up 14 of R2T2's 20 wrong clips and 20 of whisper's 23. That is a normalization artifact, not a recognition error.
- **R2T2's own error class is usted→tú register flips**, on 4 clips: "Le confirmo"→"Lo confirmo", "Su cita"→"Tu cita", "Le recomiendo"→"Te recomiendo". It also dropped one accent ("Permitame"). For an agent that must stay formal, a register flip changes meaning, which a missing accent does not.
- **Whisper's non-numeric misses** were accents only (que/qué).
- **Neither engine is real-time** offline on 2 CPU threads.
- **Caveat:** these are synthetic TTS voices. Clean TTS flatters every engine, so a pass is necessary, not sufficient. Real es-MX telephony callers are untested.

## Recommendation

Do not build anything now. Fold gate 2 into Pipesong's planned GPU window as a
**3-way A/B (R2T2 / Flux / Nova-3) on real es-MX telephony audio**, scored with:

1. **Number-normalized WER.** Map spelled-out and digit numbers to one form before scoring; otherwise the digit artifact dominates.
2. **A register-flip counter (usted→tú)** as its own metric.
3. **Time from end of speech to final transcript.** Flux's built-in end-of-turn detection is part of what R2T2 would have to replace.

Get the operator's license ruling before that window, so a pass can actually ship.
